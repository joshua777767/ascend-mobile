Rebuild and audit the entire calorie and nutrition-target system for Ascend: AI Fitness. Do not rely on previous fixes or assumptions. Trace every calculation from onboarding data to the final daily calorie and protein targets.

The goal is to produce the fastest safe and sustainable progress for both users under 18 and users 18 or older. Never use crash-diet or dirty-bulk logic.

1. Required user data

Ensure onboarding actually saves and uses:

* Date of birth or age
* Sex used for the metabolic equation
* Height
* Current weight
* Goal: lose fat, gain muscle, or maintain
* Target weight
* Desired timeframe
* Normal daily activity level
* Gym or resistance-training days
* Sports, practices, and game days
* Workout duration and intensity

Validate that these values persist after logout, login, and app restart.

2. Prevent activity double-counting

Create one clearly documented activity model.

Do not include planned gym sessions or sports inside a high activity multiplier and then add those exercise calories again.

Prefer this structure:

* Calculate BMR
* Calculate baseline daily expenditure from normal non-exercise lifestyle
* Add only the estimated calories from that day’s scheduled workout or sport
* Apply the appropriate deficit or surplus

Show and log:

* BMR
* Baseline activity calculation
* Scheduled exercise calories
* Total estimated maintenance calories
* Deficit or surplus
* Final calorie target
* Protein target
* Any safety limit or adjustment applied

3. Users age 18 or older

Fat-loss goal

Create the largest reasonable deficit that remains safe and sustainable.

* Use a percentage-based deficit instead of blindly subtracting the same amount for everyone.
* Default to approximately a 15%–20% deficit.
* Allow up to approximately 25% only for appropriate adult profiles with higher body weight and sufficient calorie intake.
* Do not let the planned rate exceed roughly 1% of body weight per week.
* Do not prescribe extremely low calorie targets.
* If the calculated target reaches a safety floor, reduce the deficit rather than forcing the target lower.
* Reassess using the user’s average weight trend over at least 14 days.

Muscle-gain goal

Use a controlled lean-gain surplus:

* Approximately 5%–10% above maintenance by default.
* Favor the lower end for experienced lifters or users already carrying substantial body fat.
* Increase calories only if body weight and gym performance fail to progress for at least two weeks.
* Avoid aggressive surpluses that mainly increase fat.

Adult protein

For users performing resistance training, calculate a reasonable protein target based on body weight or an appropriate goal-weight adjustment for users with high body fat. Keep the target within an evidence-based range and avoid unnecessarily extreme amounts.

4. Users under age 18

Use the same objective—effective fat loss or muscle gain—but apply a separate adolescent safety pathway because minors are still growing.

Do not simply run minors through the adult deficit and surplus system.

Under-18 fat-loss goal

* Use age-appropriate pediatric energy equations or estimated energy requirements rather than relying only on an adult BMR equation.
* Evaluate weight status using age- and sex-specific BMI percentile, not adult BMI categories.
* Prefer gradual fat loss, weight maintenance, or slower weight gain while height increases, depending on the user’s age and weight status.
* Do not automatically create an aggressive calorie deficit.
* Do not exceed approximately 0.5% of body weight loss per week without professional supervision.
* Block unrealistic deadlines.
* Never generate starvation-level targets.
* Require a parent/guardian and qualified healthcare professional recommendation when a minor requests aggressive weight loss, has severe obesity, is underweight, reports an eating disorder, or chooses a medically concerning target.

Under-18 muscle-gain goal

* Use a small surplus only when needed.
* Prioritize resistance training, adequate food, protein, sleep, and recovery.
* Do not create a rapid bulk.
* Consider normal growth and development when interpreting weight gain.
* Adjust calories only after at least two weeks of trend data.

Under-18 messaging

Do not display language such as “lose weight as fast as possible.”

Display:

“Your plan is designed for effective progress while supporting healthy growth and development.”

Include a notice that minors should review significant weight-change goals with a parent or guardian and healthcare professional.

5. Adaptive weekly adjustments

Use rolling weight averages, not individual weigh-ins.

* Wait for at least 14 days of reliable data before making the first major adjustment.
* If fat loss is slower than the safe target, reduce calories slightly.
* If fat loss is faster than the safe target, increase calories.
* If muscle-gain weight is not increasing, add a small calorie amount.
* If weight gain is too rapid, reduce the surplus.
* Limit each automatic adjustment to a small amount, such as 100–150 calories.
* Never adjust based on a single day.

6. Safety and validation

Flag or block:

* Underweight users attempting further weight loss
* Extreme target weights
* Extreme deadlines
* Very low calorie outputs
* Excessive deficits or surpluses
* Contradictory onboarding information
* Missing activity data
* Potential exercise double-counting

Do not diagnose medical conditions. Direct high-risk users to a physician or registered dietitian.
